## Proposed outcome

Produce one integrated `cartofreako-audit-outcomes-03` report rather than a
second pair of overlapping special-topics and audit-outcomes documents. The
new report would combine the current technical audit, the strongest material
from the local Marshall Islands special-topics draft, the reviewer feedback,
the released Stage 15 state, and an explicitly bounded assessment of
Cartofreako as a decolonial and human-first atlas practice.

The proposed central research-fit thesis is:

> Cartofreako is a decolonial cartographic and atlas practice for speculative
> exploration of Anthropocene effects. It uses projection, positioning,
> layout, and evidence layering to examine inherited representations of
> colonialism, empire, extraction, economic power, environmental change, and
> their uneven consequences.

This is a claim about the purpose, design, and documented use of the practice.
The report should distinguish that claim from an empirical finding that a
specific plate changes human belief or eliminates cognitive bias. Such an
effect remains `UNAVAILABLE` until evaluated with people through a suitable
consent-governed research design.

## Research-fit assessment to develop

### Decolonial and economic analysis

The report should assess how maps can naturalize colonial and imperial power
through centering, scale, borders, interruption, visual hierarchy, and the
treatment of oceans or islands as peripheral. Cartofreako's resource,
temperature, particulate, water-debris, reef, energy, fiber, network, and
orbital layers allow economic and environmental systems to be compared rather
than separating extraction from its infrastructures and consequences.

An alternative projection is not automatically decolonial. The practice also
depends on situated interpretation, evidence provenance, uncertainty,
consent, refusal, attribution, community authority, and accountability to the
people and places represented.

### Anthropocene boundary

“Anthropocene” should remain a critical and contested analytic term. The
report should not imply that all people or places caused planetary change
equally. It should use the atlas to ask who extracted, transported, profited,
governed, experienced exposure, or absorbed environmental consequences, while
keeping observations, models, interfaces, and speculative compositions
distinct.

### Human bias and layout

Cartofreako can demonstrably expose and alter representational defaults:
center versus edge, land versus water, continuity versus interruption,
relative scale, layer order, opacity, title hierarchy, and selected evidence.
That makes it suitable as an apparatus intended to identify and correct
representational bias. A causal claim about correcting a viewer's cognitive
bias requires controlled, participatory evaluation and should remain
`UNAVAILABLE` in the software assessment.

### `#CorrectTheMap`

The official campaign calls for Equal Earth. Cartofreako presently has no
Equal Earth implementation, so the current projection families should not be
presented as campaign-compliant substitutes. The current research fit is
therefore **strong as a reproducible comparison apparatus, conditional as a
campaign implementation, and unverified as a human-bias intervention**.

The assessment should cite the campaign and its African Union endorsement,
identify the current implementation gap, and recommend a checked Equal Earth
reference rather than claiming equivalence:

- <https://correctthemap.org/>
- <https://correctthemap.org/frequently-asked-questions/>
- <https://au.int/en/node/46520>
- <https://ica-abs.copernicus.org/articles/1/321/2019/>

### Marshall Islands boundary

For Marshall Islands research, the representational problem includes
continental centering, ocean fragmentation, peripheral island placement, and
treating water as empty background. Water-oriented Dymaxion, Pacific-centered
Myriahedral, controlled Cahill–Keyes slices, Star-X sequences, and a
source-governed local atoll tier can make different relationships inspectable.
None substitutes for local analytical coordinates, current local evidence,
Marshallese interpretation, community authorization, navigation, tenure,
inundation engineering, or policy analysis.

### Cartofreako × Alpha60 geography-aware AAO workflow

The report should assess the research fit of combining Cartofreako's
agent-assisted projection, slice, evidence, and plate selection with
Alpha60's geography-aware distribution datasets. The ongoing Alpha60
year-2024 campaign, planned ETL work, and existing `alpha60/year-2025`
archive are candidate evidence surfaces, not assumed compatible inputs.

The shared UC Berkeley Active Archive Object setting could make large public
derivatives addressable without copying them into either source repository.
The report should test what is actually demonstrated: object identity,
provenance, URL stability, cross-origin delivery, request and transfer
behavior, geographic identifiers, schema compatibility, and reproducible
selection. A common host may reduce avoidable duplication or enable reuse,
but cache-hit improvement, elapsed-time savings, bandwidth reduction, and
power reduction remain `INFERRED` or `UNAVAILABLE` unless a bounded workload
is measured.

The resulting research-fit question is whether a joined Cartofreako ×
Alpha60 workflow can support publicly inspectable, geographically situated
media analysis over large datasets for the Mellon Project. The identity,
requirements, governance, participation, adoption, and endorsement of that
project remain `UNAVAILABLE` unless supplied as evidence.

## Human-first AI-assisted atlas workflow

The report should describe Cartofreako as an AI-assisted atlas explorer with
three bounded operations:

1. **Explore:** convert a research question into one-to-five explained
   projection, slice, evidence, and plate candidates.
2. **Audit:** inspect numerics, provenance, dates, licenses, lifecycle,
   uncertainty, exclusions, and authority before treating an output as
   evidence.
3. **Report:** assemble reviewed artifacts into a claim-bounded document while
   preserving sharing and publication boundaries.

Human participants set the research question, approve evidence and external
access, interpret the cultural and political stakes, accept or reject visual
choices, and authorize release. An agent may recommend, render, compare, and
record receipts; it may not manufacture source authority, consent, community
representation, endorsement, or a finding that bias has been corrected.

The “boundary object” discussion should be attributed to **Susan Leigh Star**
and James R. Griesemer, not “Brenda Star.” Human–AI negotiation records may be
assessed as candidate boundary-object instantiations only when the report also
addresses the work arrangements, differing social worlds, scale, and
ill-structured/well-structured uses involved.

## Stage 16J — Equal Earth and positioning comparison

**Implementation update, 2026-08-11:** separately approved and completed as
an exploration-only local prototype. It adds standalone C++/JavaScript
forward and reverse support, a 30-case neutral fixture bundle checked against
PROJ 9.6.2 and D3 Geo 2.0.1, spherical distortion diagnostics, and five
matched projection-and-slice PNGs. It does not extend the six-family standard
runtime, claim campaign compliance, publish, upload, or replace the human
review proposed below.

The approved implementation scope was:

1. implement Equal Earth forward and reverse support;
2. cross-check equations and results against PROJ and D3;
3. publish implementation-neutral fixtures and area-preservation tests;
4. add local scale, angular deformation, Jacobian/Tissot, seam, and
   forward/reverse diagnostics;
5. generate matched Mercator-control, canonical Equal Earth, explicitly
   Africa-centered Equal Earth, and selected Cartofreako alternatives; and
6. hold data, dimensions, colors, labels, and extent constant while changing
   one named projection or layout variable at a time.

The completed prototype preserves the estimate's separate human cartographic
review gate. Its `11.5°E` centered variant is explicitly labeled experimental
and is not labeled EPSG:8857.

## Stage 17 candidate — participatory verification

Testing whether projection and layout change human perception belongs in a
separate research stage. A possible study would measure area estimation,
position recall, salience, perceived connectivity, task accuracy, confidence,
and interpretive response across randomized matched plates. It should record
relevant background and map literacy, isolate design variables where
possible, and include privacy, consent, withdrawal, attribution, and community
governance.

This stage would require African, Marshallese, Pacific, critical-cartography,
or other situated collaborators appropriate to the question. It is likely a
**4–8 week or longer elapsed program**, depending on collaborator availability
and institutional research requirements, rather than an unattended software
task.
